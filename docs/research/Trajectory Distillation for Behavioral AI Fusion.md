# Beyond Parameter Averaging: A Comparative Feasibility Study of Trajectory Distillation for Behavioral AI Fusion

## Technical Viability of Trajectory Distillation Methodologies

The concept of "Trajectory Distillation" does not represent a single, monolithic algorithm but rather an umbrella term for a class of advanced training-free or post-training techniques that transfer knowledge by distilling multi-step reasoning processes, or "trajectories," rather than from static datasets or final outputs [[6](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26778v1), [7](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00305v2)]. This paradigm shift from distilling end-states to distilling processes offers a powerful mechanism for refining the behavioral capabilities of artificial intelligence systems, particularly those requiring complex, sequential reasoning. An analysis of academic literature reveals several distinct yet related methodologies that fall under this banner, each with demonstrated technical feasibility in both academic and industrial contexts. These approaches fundamentally differ from traditional knowledge distillation by focusing on correcting the path of reasoning itself, making them exceptionally well-suited for tasks involving behavioral fusion.

One prominent paradigm is On-Policy Distillation (OPD), a post-training technique where a student model learns from its own self-generated reasoning trajectories under the supervision of a teacher model [[7](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00305v2)]. This methodology has been validated in significant industrial applications, including the alignment pipelines for DeepSeek-V4, MiMo, and Qwen-3, underscoring its real-world relevance [[7](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00305v2)]. The core challenge addressed by OPD is the mismatch between its token-level learning signal and the inherently multi-step nature of complex reasoning, which can lead to issues like "token-by-token learning trap" [[7](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00305v2)]. To overcome this, its advanced variant, Trajectory-aware OPD (TOPD), introduces a mechanism to identify genuinely divergent states within a reasoning path by comparing short-window continuations using Optimal Transport (OT) [[7](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00305v2)]. By injecting information about the near-future trajectory into the learning objective, TOPD provides a more effective corrective signal, enabling the student model to repair its reasoning drift. Empirical results demonstrate its superiority over standard OPD, with accuracy improvements on competitive mathematics benchmarks like AIME25 increasing from 46.7% to 53.3% [[7](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00305v2)]. This approach is technically viable, though its primary challenge lies in managing noisy supervision signals; ablation studies confirm that downweighting "false alarms"—tokens that appear problematic but correspond to benign surface-form mismatches—is crucial for performance [[7](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00305v2)].

A second, distinct paradigm is exemplified by Training-Free Experience Distillation (TED), which reformulates knowledge distillation entirely away from parameter updates [[6](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26778v1)]. In this framework, the student model's parameters are frozen. Instead, the teacher model evaluates multiple parallel reasoning trajectories generated by the student for a given input, compares them against its own correct path, and extracts generalized, reusable "experiences." These experiences are not raw data points but higher-level reasoning principles, strategies, failure patterns, and correction rules [[6](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26778v1)]. The distilled experience is then stored in the student's context window at inference time to guide future responses without any need for retraining. The feasibility of TED is particularly high in resource-constrained environments, such as edge devices or black-box API scenarios, where parameter updates are impractical [[6](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26778v1)]. It dramatically reduces training costs, achieving a performance improvement on the MathVision benchmark while reducing training cost by over 20x compared to conventional parameter-based distillation [[6](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26778v1)]. The main challenge for this approach is preventing unbounded growth and noise accumulation in the distilled experience set. TED addresses this through a teacher-guided experience compression mechanism that selectively merges, rewriting, or removing low-utility experiences to maintain a compact and informative context [[6](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26778v1)].

A third paradigm involves distilling the collective dynamics of multi-agent systems into a single, more capable agent. The AgentArk framework demonstrates this principle, shifting the computational burden from inference to a pre-training phase [[8](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03955v1)]. The goal is to equip a single LLM with the sophisticated reasoning abilities of a collaborative team of specialized agents [[8](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03955v1)]. AgentArk implements several distillation strategies, with Process-Aware Distillation (PAD) identified as the most effective. PAD uses a Process Reward Model (PRM) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to perform step-level reinforcement learning based on high-signal, corrective reasoning patterns extracted from multi-agent debates [[8](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03955v1)]. This method consistently yields performance improvements and qualitatively enhances reasoning quality in areas like step decomposition and intermediate verification [[8](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03955v1)]. The technical feasibility of this approach is established, albeit with a significant trade-off: a substantial upfront offline training cost. For instance, training a 7B parameter student model required approximately 20 hours on 8 NVIDIA H100 GPUs [[8](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03955v1)]. However, this investment pays off by producing a model with enhanced generalization, robustness, and reduced inference-time cost. Critically, the framework shows that the distilled reasoning patterns are model-agnostic, as successfully transferring reasoning skills from a text-only model to a smaller Multimodal LLM (MLLM) indicates the capture of transferable, abstract reasoning principles [[8](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03955v1)].

In summary, the various forms of Trajectory Distillation are not only technically plausible but have been implemented and validated in demanding real-world scenarios. Their strength lies in their ability to directly address and refine the behavioral aspects of an AI model—the "how" of its reasoning—rather than just its static knowledge or final output. Whether through on-policy self-correction, lightweight inference guidance, or the synthesis of collaborative intelligence, these methods provide powerful tools for building more capable and coherent AI systems.

## A Landscape of Challenges in Parameter-Space Model Merging

Parameter-Space Merge, also known as model merging or fusion, is the practice of combining the parameters of multiple independently trained neural networks into a single, unified model without requiring further training [[19](https://medium.com/@j13mehul/from-fine-tuning-to-fusion-the-role-of-agentic-ai-in-model-merging-f695a4252447), [21](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09938v2)]. At its core, the technique relies on the assumption that different models, when fine-tuned from a common pre-trained backbone on related tasks, occupy a "connected" region in the high-dimensional loss landscape [[4](https://cameronrwolfe.substack.com/p/model-merging), [5](https://ritvik19.medium.com/papers-explained-review-13-model-merging-d0db49797b90)]. Within this connected region, simple interpolation methods like linear averaging of weights can produce a new model that inherits and often enhances the capabilities of its predecessors [[4](https://cameronrwolfe.substack.com/p/model-merging)]. This concept is supported by theoretical underpinnings such as "mode connectivity," which suggests that different minima (i.e., trained models) are linked by a path of low loss, and "sparsity," which posits that many network parameters are redundant, thereby reducing the likelihood of destructive interference during merging [[4](https://cameronrwolfe.substack.com/p/model-merging)]. Despite this seemingly straightforward premise, the practical application of parameter merging is a landscape fraught with significant challenges, as evidenced by a growing body of academic research highlighting the fragility of these techniques, especially when applied to modern Large Language Models (LLMs).

The foundational technique, Model Soups, proposed by Wortsman et al., involves training multiple identical models with different hyperparameters and then creating a final model by averaging their weights [[4](https://cameronrwolfe.substack.com/p/model-merging)]. This method was shown to achieve state-of-the-art performance on ImageNet and serves as a baseline for more complex algorithms [[4](https://cameronrwolfe.substack.com/p/model-merging)]. While simple, its success depends heavily on the models being variants of the same architecture fine-tuned on related tasks, where the assumption of a connected parameter space holds reasonably well [[5](https://ritvik19.medium.com/papers-explained-review-13-model-merging-d0db49797b90)]. However, when models are trained for different purposes or on heterogeneous data, this assumption often fails. A large-scale systematic evaluation of six state-of-the-art merging methods found that only Task Arithmetic—a technique treating fine-tuning updates as "task vectors" for algebraic combination—consistently yielded performance gains [[3](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.21437v1)]. Other advanced, interference-aware algorithms, such as TIES-Merging (Trim, Elect Sign & Merge), frequently resulted in significant performance drops [[3](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.21437v1), [4](https://cameronrwolfe.substack.com/p/model-merging)]. TIES-Merging, designed to resolve interference by retaining only influential parameters and resolving sign conflicts, suffered from catastrophic performance degradation when merging heterogeneous checkpoints, with its success probability dropping to zero as the number of models increased [[3](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.21437v1)]. The authors hypothesize this is due to moving too far away in weight space, causing catastrophic forgetting of learned skills [[3](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.21437v1)]. Similarly, subspace-boosted merging methods like TSV-Merge and Iso-C, which compress updates into low-rank directions, also failed to produce constructive interference on LLMs, suggesting their underlying assumptions about coherent update directions do not hold for diverse, randomly sampled public checkpoints [[3](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.21437v1)].

To address some of the limitations of flat-space averaging, researchers have developed geometric interpolation methods. Spherical Linear Interpolation (SLERP) performs interpolation along a great circle on the sphere of normalized weight vectors, preserving angular relationships and keeping the merged model "on the manifold" [[5](https://ritvik19.medium.com/papers-explained-review-13-model-merging-d0db49797b90), [19](https://medium.com/@j13mehul/from-fine-tuning-to-fusion-the-role-of-agentic-ai-in-model-merging-f695a4252447)]. A normalized version, NuSLERP, explicitly normalizes weight vectors before interpolation to handle models with different training scales [[5](https://ritvik19.medium.com/papers-explained-review-13-model-merging-d0db49797b90)]. While potentially more stable than linear interpolation, these geometric methods are computationally expensive due to the angle computations they require and may not be compatible with all model architectures [[19](https://medium.com/@j13mehul/from-fine-tuning-to-fusion-the-role-of-agentic-ai-in-model-merging-f695a4252447)]. Another approach, Nearswap, attempts to be more intelligent by partitioning model parameters and swapping or averaging only those regions where two models are deemed "close" (i.e., their difference is below a threshold τ) [[5](https://ritvik19.medium.com/papers-explained-review-13-model-merging-d0db49797b90)]. While innovative, its effectiveness is contingent on accurately identifying these similar regions, which is not always straightforward.

Recognizing that naive merging can destroy valuable, task-specific skills, a new generation of alignment-focused merging frameworks has emerged. AlignMerge represents a significant conceptual advance by treating alignment (e.g., safety, helpfulness) as a geometric invariant to be explicitly preserved during the fusion process [[2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16245)]. Instead of operating in a flat parameter space, AlignMerge defines an "alignment subspace" using a Fisher metric derived from an aligned base model. It then formulates the merge as a geometry-constrained optimization problem that pulls the merged model towards expert checkpoints along a Riemannian barycenter path while penalizing motion along directions sensitive to alignment degradation [[2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16245)]. By using the Alignment Quality Index (AQI) as an explicit penalty term, AlignMerge can detect subtle degradation in alignment even when surface metrics remain stable. Evaluations across five major model families showed that AlignMerge consistently improved alignment metrics while matching or exceeding the best expert on utility tasks, demonstrating smaller alignment-subspace drift and fewer violations of a soft alignment budget compared to baselines like SafeMerge and MergeAlign [[2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16245)]. This approach is particularly relevant for behavioral fusion, as it provides a principled way to combine models with different strengths while preserving critical safety or ethical behaviors. Furthermore, its framework is inherently multimodal, capable of being extended to Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by defining joint Fisher metrics on mixed text-image data [[2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16245)].

Finally, an alternative to direct fusion is Passthrough Merge, which avoids destructive interference altogether by not combining models at the weight level [[19](https://medium.com/@j13mehul/from-fine-tuning-to-fusion-the-role-of-agentic-ai-in-model-merging-f695a4252447)]. Instead, it creates a dynamic routing mechanism that directs incoming queries to the most suitable model at inference time. Goliath-120B was built using this method with two Llama models via Mergekit [[19](https://medium.com/@j13mehul/from-fine-tuning-to-fusion-the-role-of-agentic-ai-in-model-merging-f695a4252447)]. While this approach effectively sidesteps the risk of performance degradation from merging conflicting models, it introduces a new set of challenges. The primary limitation is potential latency issues; the dynamic decision-making and processing delays introduced by the routing mechanism may not be suitable for latency-sensitive applications like chatbots or autonomous systems [[19](https://medium.com/@j13mehul/from-fine-tuning-to-fusion-the-role-of-agentic-ai-in-model-merging-f695a4252447)]. It also requires running multiple models, thus not reducing the overall computational overhead in the same way a fused model would.

In conclusion, the technical feasibility of parameter-space merging is highly conditional. While simple averaging can work under controlled conditions, merging complex, independently developed models is a high-risk endeavor that often leads to performance degradation. The failure of many advanced, interference-aware methods on modern LLMs suggests that the underlying assumptions about task disentanglement and coherent update directions are frequently violated in practice [[3](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.21437v1)]. Advanced techniques like AlignMerge offer a promising path forward by explicitly modeling and preserving critical attributes like alignment, making them more robust for sensitive applications. However, for many practical scenarios, the risks associated with parameter merging may outweigh the benefits, leading to alternatives like Passthrough Merge or a strategic avoidance of merging altogether.

## Comparative Analysis: Behavioral Refinement vs. Capability Aggregation

The choice between Trajectory Distillation and Parameter-Space Merge for building advanced AI systems hinges on a fundamental distinction in their primary objectives: behavioral refinement versus capability aggregation. Trajectory Distillation methodologies are primarily concerned with teaching a model *how to reason* correctly and coherently over a sequence of steps. They excel at refining the behavioral aspects of an AI, ensuring that its actions are logically sound, contextually appropriate, and consistent throughout a complex interaction. In contrast, Parameter-Space Merge is fundamentally a technique for aggregating disparate skills and knowledge sets from different models into a single, unified architecture. It aims to create a broader, more versatile model by combining the distinct capabilities of its components. Understanding this dichotomy is crucial for determining which approach—or combination thereof—is most suitable for a given task, particularly in the nuanced domain of empathetic and multimodal AI.

Trajectory Distillation's strength lies in its ability to address the "process" of intelligence, not just the "product." Methods like TOPD directly tackle the "token-by-token learning trap" inherent in standard OPD, where single-token corrections are often insufficient to repair a deeper reasoning flaw that manifests over multiple steps [[7](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00305v2)]. By evaluating entire conversational or reasoning trajectories, these methods can identify where a model first deviates from a correct path and provide a more holistic corrective signal [[7](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00305v2)]. This is precisely the kind of control needed to shape complex behaviors. For an empathetic AI system, this means ensuring that the model doesn't just generate an empathetic keyword at one point but maintains an appropriate emotional tone and supportive stance throughout a multi-turn dialogue. The X-OPD framework provides a clear example of this power in action, using a text-based "teacher" to evaluate and align the reasoning trajectories of a speech-based "student" model [[10](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24596v1)]. This cross-modal alignment is achieved by bridging the logic gap between the teacher's text-based reasoning and the student's speech-conditioned output, demonstrating how trajectory evaluation can enforce behavioral consistency across different modalities [[10](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24596v1)]. Similarly, the TED framework teaches a model to extract and apply generalized reasoning principles, which allows it to adapt its behavior to novel situations without needing to be explicitly retrained on every possible scenario [[6](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26778v1)]. The core value proposition of Trajectory Distillation is therefore behavioral fidelity and composability.

On the other hand, Parameter-Space Merge is optimized for capability aggregation. Its goal is to construct a single model that possesses a wider range of skills than any of its individual components. For example, one could envision having a model expert in factual knowledge retrieval, another proficient in sentiment analysis, and a third skilled in generating creative prose. Merging these three models could, in theory, create a single, more comprehensive assistant. This approach is effective when the desired outcome is a broad knowledge base or a collection of distinct functionalities. However, as extensive research shows, simply merging models can be disastrous. Naive merging can lead to catastrophic forgetting, where the very skills one wishes to preserve are erased [[3](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.21437v1), [10](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24596v1)]. The success of merging is highly dependent on the similarity of the tasks the models were trained on and the sophistication of the merging algorithm. Techniques like AlignMerge attempt to mitigate this by explicitly preserving alignment, but the fundamental risk remains high [[2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16245)]. Therefore, Parameter-Space Merge is best suited for scenarios where the models being combined have complementary, non-conflicting skill sets, or when advanced techniques are used to carefully manage the fusion process.

Given these distinct strengths, the most effective strategy for building a sophisticated AI system may not be to choose one method over the other, but to employ them synergistically in a multi-stage process. A hybrid approach could leverage the strengths of both paradigms. The first stage would involve using a robust Parameter-Space Merge technique, such as AlignMerge, to build a foundational model by aggregating core capabilities. For an empathetic agent, this could involve fusing a general-purpose LLM with models specialized in emotional recognition, personality modeling, and factual knowledge. Using an alignment-preserving method ensures that the nuanced skills related to empathy and helpfulness are retained during this aggregation phase [[2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16245)]. The second stage would then apply a Trajectory Distillation technique, like TOPD or X-OPD, to this newly formed foundation. This refinement step would teach the aggregated model *how to behave* cohesively. It would ensure that the factual knowledge is presented with the appropriate emotional sensitivity, that the personality model informs the tone of the conversation appropriately, and that the agent can navigate complex, multi-step interactions without losing its behavioral thread. This two-pronged strategy combines the broad capability-building of parameter merging with the precise behavioral tuning of trajectory distillation, offering a pathway to creating AI systems that are both knowledgeable and comport themselves intelligently.

| **Suitability for Behavior** | Excellent for shaping nuanced, context-dependent behaviors like empathy, politeness, and logical consistency [[10](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24596v1), [22](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12594504/)]. | Poor for behavioral shaping unless alignment is explicitly preserved; better for combining hard skills [[2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16245)]. |

This comparative analysis reveals that the two methodologies are not direct competitors but rather complementary tools in the AI development toolkit. Trajectory Distillation offers a surgical instrument for honing the behavioral aspects of a model, while Parameter-Space Merge acts more like a construction crane for assembling a wide array of capabilities. For the ambitious goal of creating truly empathetic and multimodal AI, a strategy that employs both—a robust merger of capabilities followed by a meticulous refinement of behavior—appears to be the most promising path forward.

## Application in Multimodal AI Systems

The integration of multiple sensory inputs, such as text, audio, and visual data, is a cornerstone of developing advanced AI systems. Both Trajectory Distillation and Parameter-Space Merge offer pathways to fuse behaviors and capabilities within these multimodal contexts, although their mechanisms and challenges differ significantly. Evidence from academic research and emerging industry practices indicates that these techniques are not only applicable but are being actively explored to build more cohesive and capable multimodal agents.

Parameter-Space Merge in multimodal systems faces the critical challenge of preserving modality-specific integrity while creating a unified representation. A study on fusing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with Language Models (LLMs) provides a stark illustration of this difficulty. The research found that naively merging all parameters of the two models led to suboptimal performance, suggesting that the fusion process can dilute the specialized visual processing capabilities of the VLM, which are essential for certain visual-textual reasoning tasks [[1](https://necou.github.io/research/2025/03/13/multimodal-fusion.html)]. However, the study discovered a more effective strategy: selectively merging only the self-attention tensors in each layer while preserving the VLM's original embedding layers. This selective approach significantly outperformed global weight merging, achieving a 25.46% accuracy on the MATH-Vision dataset and 69.8% on Math-Vista mini [[1](https://necou.github.io/research/2025/03/13/multimodal-fusion.html)]. This finding underscores a crucial insight: successful fusion in multimodal systems may require surgical precision rather than wholesale averaging. The optimal configuration identified a moderate weight balance, with the VLM maintaining greater influence (a 60% VLM + 40% LLM influence ratio), reinforcing the idea that diluting a model's core competency can be detrimental [[1](https://necou.github.io/research/2025/03/13/multimodal-fusion.html)]. The AlignMerge framework is particularly relevant here, as it is inherently multimodal and could be applied to ensure that merging a vision model with a language model does not inadvertently degrade the vision capabilities [[2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16245)]. By defining a joint Fisher metric on mixed text-image data, AlignMerge could help navigate the complex parameter space of a multimodal model, preserving alignment and preventing the loss of critical perceptual skills.

Trajectory Distillation, in contrast, offers a more process-oriented approach to multimodal fusion, focusing on aligning the reasoning and behavior across different modalities. The X-OPD (Cross-Modal On-Policy Distillation) framework is a prime example of this paradigm in action [[10](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24596v1)]. It was designed to address the "performance degradation" observed in End-to-End (E2E) speech-based LLMs when handling complex tasks, a phenomenon noted in flagship systems like GPT-4o and Gemini [[10](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24596v1)]. X-OPD uses a powerful text-based "teacher" model to evaluate the self-generated reasoning trajectories of a speech-based "student" model. The teacher scores these trajectories token-by-token, providing a detailed learning signal that bridges the logic gap between the teacher's superior text-based reasoning and the student's speech-conditioned output [[10](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24596v1)]. This on-policy approach allows the student to learn from its own mistakes and refine its reasoning process in a way that is directly tied to the teacher's superior understanding. The results were significant, with X-OPD reducing the average score drop on the BIG Bench Audio benchmark from 11.29% (SFT baseline) to just 0.97% [[10](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24596v1)]. This demonstrates the feasibility of using trajectory distillation to align the behavioral outputs of models operating in different modalities, ensuring that the reasoning process is consistent regardless of whether the input is spoken or written.

Furthermore, research into multimodal dataset distillation highlights another challenge: "Modality Collapse," where distilled datasets exhibit a large inter-modal gap due to overly strong cross-modal supervision [[11](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14705v1)]. The RepBlend framework was proposed to counteract this by introducing "Representation Blending" to weaken dominant features and "Symmetric Projection Trajectory Matching" to balance the optimization trajectories of modality-specific projection heads [[11](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14705v1)]. This focus on balancing the learning *trajectory* across modalities is a clear nod to the principles of trajectory-based methods, suggesting that controlling the optimization path is key to achieving balanced and effective multimodal fusion. The AgentArk framework further supports the applicability of distillation principles to multimodal systems. Experiments showed that reasoning traces distilled from a text-only model could be effectively transferred to a smaller MLLM, indicating that the framework captures model-agnostic reasoning patterns that are not tied to a specific modality [[8](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03955v1)]. This implies that once a model learns a general reasoning structure through distillation, it can be adapted to operate on multimodal inputs, opening up possibilities for creating versatile multimodal agents from more easily trained text-only teachers.

In practice, industry projects are beginning to showcase the potential of multimodal empathetic agents. Google's sample project for a customer service agent named 'Cymbal Home & Garden' is designed to be friendly, empathetic, and efficient by combining text and video inputs [[16](https://github.com/google/adk-samples/blob/main/python/agents/customer-service/README.md)]. It leverages the Gemini model to understand customer needs and can provide personalized assistance, send care instructions, and manage orders, all while simulating a rich interactive experience [[16](https://github.com/google/adk-samples/blob/main/python/agents/customer-service/README.md)]. While the internal mechanics are not detailed, the architecture is described as multi-modal, combining text and video [[16](https://github.com/google/adk-samples/blob/main/python/agents/customer-service/README.md)]. Similarly, the madhavappaneni/Empathetic-Conversational-Chatbot project aims to create an end-to-end system capable of delivering empathetic interactions, representing an effort in the open-source community to build such systems [[13](https://github.com/madhavappaneni/Empathetic-Conversational-Chatbot)]. Although these projects do not explicitly detail the use of parameter merging or trajectory distillation, they establish a clear market demand and architectural direction for multimodal empathetic AI. The technical feasibility of the underlying fusion techniques provides a strong foundation for the development of such systems, with parameter merging offering a path to integrate disparate sensing and reasoning modules, and trajectory distillation providing a mechanism to ensure the resulting agent behaves cohesively and empathetically across all modalities.

## Implementation in Empathetic and Behavioral AI Systems

Creating artificial intelligence systems capable of exhibiting empathy and other complex human-like behaviors requires more than just vast amounts of data; it demands sophisticated techniques for shaping and refining model behavior. Both Trajectory Distillation and Parameter-Space Merge present viable, albeit distinct, pathways for achieving this goal. The literature provides compelling evidence of their application in this domain, from fine-tuning models on psychological principles to using advanced distillation frameworks to teach the process of empathetic reasoning.

Parameter-Space Merge can be employed to aggregate different facets of an empathetic persona. One could imagine having a base LLM, a model fine-tuned specifically for sentiment analysis, and another trained to recognize evaluative expressions of emotion (e.g., 'betrayed', 'belittled') and map them to associated feelings and unmet needs [[17](https://github.com/energycombined/empathyondemand)]. Merging these models could theoretically create a single agent with a richer, more integrated capacity for emotional understanding. However, the risks are substantial. An ablation study within the X-OPD framework revealed that standard Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and other alignment methods could cause severe catastrophic forgetting of previously acquired skills, dropping accuracy from 71.3% to 59.9% on the MMAR benchmark [[10](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24596v1)]. This highlights the danger of assuming that simply combining models will yield a synergistic result. To make merging viable for empathetic systems, alignment-preserving techniques like AlignMerge are essential. By treating the preservation of empathetic and helpful behaviors as a geometric constraint during the merge, such frameworks could potentially combine a model's general knowledge with its specific empathetic skills without compromising the latter [[2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16245)]. Industry examples like Solace AI, an "empathetic mental health companion," suggest that such systems are being developed, likely relying on fine-tuning and potentially other fusion techniques to achieve their desired behavioral profile [[14](https://github.com/Rayyan9477/Solace-AI)].

Trajectory Distillation, however, offers a more direct and controllable method for refining empathetic behavior. Instead of trying to merge static representations of empathy, this approach teaches the *process* of being empathetic. An empathetic agent can be trained by having a teacher model (which could be a human-in-the-loop or a more advanced AI) evaluate the conversational trajectories of the student model. The teacher would score the student's responses not just on content but on qualities like appropriateness, depth of understanding, and consistency of tone over a multi-turn exchange. This scoring function becomes the basis for a refined learning signal. The TED framework, for example, could be adapted to distill principles of empathetic communication into the model's context, guiding its future interactions without altering its core parameters [[6](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26778v1)]. The qualitative benefits of such an approach are significant. As noted in research, the potential of LLMs to enhance user engagement through empathy is a key area of interest [[22](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12594504/)]. Frameworks like TOPD, which can identify and correct reasoning drift, would be invaluable for ensuring that an empathetic agent does not lose its empathetic "thread" mid-conversation, a common failure mode in simpler chatbots [[7](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00305v2)]. The creation of a dataset linking evaluative terms to emotions and needs, in collaboration with psychologists, grounds these efforts in established psychological principles, providing a solid foundation for training and evaluation [[17](https://github.com/energycombined/empathyondemand)].

The distinction between these methods is crucial. Parameter merging is about assembly, while trajectory distillation is about coaching. For building a comprehensive empathetic agent, a hybrid strategy appears most promising. First, one could use an advanced merging technique like AlignMerge to combine a powerful base LLM with models specializing in different aspects of emotional intelligence (e.g., recognizing facial expressions from a video feed, interpreting tone from audio). This creates a capability-rich foundation. Second, one would apply a trajectory distillation method like X-OPD or a custom TOPD variant to coach this fused model. The coaching would involve evaluating the agent's full conversational trajectories against a gold-standard empathetic response path, correcting not just individual words but the overall arc of the interaction. This two-stage process leverages the efficiency of parameter merging for capability aggregation and the precision of trajectory distillation for behavioral refinement. Research into evaluating persona-aligned empathy through trajectory-level psychological metrics, such as the EMPA metric, provides a formal basis for scoring these trajectories, making the distillation process measurable and repeatable [[20](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.06194v3)]. Ultimately, the goal is to move beyond keyword-triggered empathy to a system that understands and navigates emotional complexity, a task for which trajectory-based methods are uniquely suited.

## Synthesis and Strategic Recommendations

This deep research report has conducted a high-level technical feasibility assessment of the Trajectory Distillation method and compared it with alternative fusion approaches, notably Parameter-Space Merge, within the context of multimodal and empathetic AI systems. The analysis, synthesized from a wide range of academic literature and industry reports, reveals that both methodologies are technically plausible but serve fundamentally different purposes and carry distinct sets of challenges and opportunities. The choice between them—or, more strategically, their combined application—is critical for designing next-generation AI systems capable of sophisticated behavioral fusion.

The investigation confirms that "Trajectory Distillation" is not a singular technique but a family of methods focused on refining a model's reasoning process by distilling knowledge from entire multi-step trajectories [[6](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26778v1), [7](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00305v2)]. These approaches, including On-Policy Distillation (TOPD) and Training-Free Experience Distillation (TED), have demonstrated high feasibility in industrial applications and research settings [[6](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26778v1), [7](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00305v2)]. Their primary strength lies in behavioral refinement; they excel at teaching an AI system *how to reason* coherently and appropriately, making them exceptionally well-suited for cultivating nuanced behaviors like empathy and ensuring consistency in complex interactions [[7](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00305v2), [10](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24596v1)]. The core mechanism involves a teacher model evaluating the student's reasoning path and providing a corrective signal that addresses logical flaws and reasoning drift, a significant advantage over token-by-token learning methods [[7](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00305v2)]. However, this feasibility comes with caveats: these methods often require a teacher model, can be computationally intensive during the training phase, and necessitate careful management of supervision signals to avoid being misled by superficial errors [[6](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26778v1), [7](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00305v2)].

Conversely, Parameter-Space Merge, or model merging, is a technique for aggregating the capabilities of multiple models into a single entity by combining their weights [[19](https://medium.com/@j13mehul/from-fine-tuning-to-fusion-the-role-of-agentic-ai-in-model-merging-f695a4252447), [21](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09938v2)]. While conceptually appealing for its simplicity, its real-world feasibility is precarious. Foundational assumptions, such as models occupying a connected region in parameter space, frequently break down when merging heterogeneous models, leading to a high risk of performance degradation and catastrophic forgetting [[3](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.21437v1), [12](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.13992v1)]. Standard techniques like TIES-Merging have been shown to fail catastrophically, and even advanced geometric methods like SLERP introduce significant computational overhead [[3](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.21437v1), [19](https://medium.com/@j13mehul/from-fine-tuning-to-fusion-the-role-of-agentic-ai-in-model-merging-f695a4252447)]. The notable exception is the emergence of alignment-focused frameworks like AlignMerge, which treats alignment as a geometric invariant to be explicitly preserved during the merge [[2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16245)]. This represents a significant advancement, making parameter merging a more viable option for combining models where critical attributes like safety or empathy must be retained [[2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16245)]. Yet, even with these advanced techniques, the process remains risky compared to trajectory distillation's targeted behavioral refinement.

For the specific goals of building multimodal and empathetic AI systems, the strategic implications are clear. Trajectory Distillation emerges as the superior tool for behavioral fusion. Its ability to evaluate and correct entire reasoning paths makes it ideal for ensuring that an empathetic agent maintains its supportive stance throughout a conversation or that a multimodal agent's reasoning is consistent across text, audio, and visual inputs [[8](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03955v1), [10](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24596v1)]. Parameter-Space Merge, while useful for initial capability aggregation, carries a high risk of destroying the very nuanced skills it is intended to combine. Selective merging of specific tensor types has shown promise in preserving modality-specific competencies, but this requires careful engineering [[1](https://necou.github.io/research/2025/03/13/multimodal-fusion.html)].

Therefore, the most robust and forward-looking strategy is a hybrid, two-stage approach:

1.  **Capability Aggregation via Robust Merging:** Begin by using an advanced parameter-space merging technique, preferably one that explicitly preserves alignment like AlignMerge [[2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16245)]. This stage's goal is to assemble a foundational model by combining disparate but complementary skills—for instance, general knowledge, factual recall, and emotional recognition—into a single, more versatile architecture.
2.  **Behavioral Refinement via Trajectory Distillation:** Following the merge, subject the newly created foundation model to a trajectory distillation process. Employ a framework like TOPD or X-OPD, using a teacher model to evaluate the student's full conversational or reasoning trajectories [[7](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00305v2), [10](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24596v1)]. This stage focuses on refining the model's behavior, teaching it to compose its aggregated capabilities into coherent, contextually appropriate, and empathetic actions.

This synergistic strategy leverages the strengths of both paradigms: the efficiency of parameter merging for building breadth of capability and the precision of trajectory distillation for instilling depth of behavioral quality. By adopting this structured approach, developers can navigate the complexities of model fusion and move closer to realizing the potential of truly intelligent, empathetic, and multimodal artificial agents.
